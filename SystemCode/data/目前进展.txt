## Evaluation

FleetCare AI was evaluated at three levels: **multiclass predictive performance, maintenance-risk ranking, and hybrid reasoning effectiveness**. Because the validation dataset is highly imbalanced, with 4,910 of 5,046 vehicles belonging to C0, overall classification accuracy alone is not an appropriate measure of system performance. The evaluation therefore places greater emphasis on minority-class performance, risk ranking, and the ability to identify critical vehicles under constrained inspection capacity.

### 1. Multiclass Predictive Performance

The final XGBoost model predicts five repair-proximity classes ranging from C0, representing no predicted repair within 48 operational units, to C4, representing repair proximity within 0–6 operational units.

| Class | Precision | Recall | F1-score | Support |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| C0 | 0.9780 | 0.8069 | 0.8843 | 4,910 |
| C1 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 16 |
| C2 | 0.0054 | 0.2143 | 0.0105 | 14 |
| C3 | 0.0139 | 0.0333 | 0.0196 | 30 |
| C4 | 0.0654 | 0.2237 | 0.1012 | 76 |

The model achieved an overall accuracy of **78.93%**, a macro F1-score of **0.2031**, and a weighted F1-score of **0.8621**.

However, these results must be interpreted in the context of severe class imbalance. Approximately 97.3% of validation vehicles belong to C0, while C1–C4 collectively account for only 136 vehicles. Consequently, overall accuracy and weighted F1 are dominated by C0 performance and do not adequately represent the system's ability to detect rare maintenance events.

The low performance for C1–C3 also indicates that exact five-class repair-proximity classification remains difficult. Therefore, FleetCare does not rely solely on the hard predicted class for maintenance decisions. Instead, the class probability outputs are also transformed into cumulative maintenance-risk scores and used as ranking signals.

### 2. Maintenance-Risk Ranking

Four cumulative risk horizons were evaluated:

- **Risk48:** repair within 48 operational units
- **Risk24:** repair within 24 operational units
- **Risk12:** repair within 12 operational units
- **Risk6:** repair within 6 operational units

The raw XGBoost outputs are treated as **model risk scores rather than calibrated real-world repair probabilities**, since calibration experiments did not improve the final model performance.

| Risk Horizon | Average Precision | ROC-AUC | Top-5% Recall | Top-10% Recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Risk48 | 0.0518 | 0.6929 | 0.0882 | 0.1765 |
| Risk24 | 0.0467 | 0.6665 | 0.1083 | 0.1667 |
| Risk12 | 0.0444 | 0.6708 | 0.1321 | 0.2264 |
| Risk6 | 0.0443 | **0.7111** | **0.1974** | **0.2763** |

Risk6 achieved the strongest ROC-AUC of **0.7111**, indicating that although exact multiclass classification is challenging, the model retains useful discriminatory information for identifying vehicles approaching critical repair conditions.

When vehicles were ranked solely by Risk6, inspecting the highest-risk 5% of the fleet identified **19.74% of all C4 vehicles**, while inspecting the highest-risk 10% identified **27.63%**.

This supports the use of the predictive model as a **maintenance-risk ranking mechanism**, rather than relying exclusively on its hard C0–C4 classification.

### 3. Temporal Rule Engine Evaluation

The predictive model was subsequently combined with a temporal reasoning layer. The rule engine considers multiple sources of evidence, including current model state, fleet-relative risk, historical risk persistence, relative deterioration, class trajectory, prediction stability, and history sufficiency.

The resulting maintenance actions were evaluated against the true repair outcomes.

| Intervention | Fleet Selected | C4 Recall | C4 Precision | Risk48 Recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Priority Inspect | 3.47% | 17.11% | 7.43% | 10.29% |
| Inspect or Higher | 6.70% | 27.63% | 6.21% | 17.65% |
| Enhanced Monitor or Higher | 18.99% | 34.21% | 2.71% | 30.15% |

The **Inspect or Higher** policy selected only 6.7% of the fleet while identifying 27.63% of all C4 vehicles. This demonstrates that the temporal reasoning layer can concentrate critical vehicles into a relatively small inspection group.

The rule engine is not intended to replace the predictive model or act as a second classifier. Instead, its purpose is to translate predictive and longitudinal evidence into transparent maintenance actions such as `PRIORITY_INSPECT`, `INSPECT`, `ENHANCED_MONITOR`, `MONITOR`, and `CONTINUE`.

### 4. Baseline vs Hybrid Reasoning

To determine whether temporal reasoning provides additional value beyond the predictive model, the final hybrid ranking was compared against a baseline in which vehicles were ranked solely according to the XGBoost Risk6 score.

Both methods were evaluated using exactly the same inspection capacities.

| Inspection Capacity | Baseline C4 Recall | Hybrid C4 Recall | Baseline C4 Precision | Hybrid C4 Precision |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2.5% | 9.21% | **13.16%** | 5.51% | **7.87%** |
| 5.0% | 19.74% | **21.05%** | 5.93% | **6.32%** |
| 7.5% | **26.32%** | 25.00% | **5.28%** | 5.01% |
| 10.0% | 27.63% | **28.95%** | 4.16% | **4.36%** |
| 15.0% | 38.16% | 38.16% | 3.83% | 3.83% |
| 20.0% | **47.37%** | 46.05% | **3.56%** | 3.47% |

The results show that the hybrid reasoning system does **not uniformly outperform** the raw ML ranking across all inspection capacities. Instead, its primary benefit appears under constrained inspection capacity.

At the most selective 2.5% inspection level, the baseline identified 7 C4 vehicles, whereas the hybrid system identified 10. C4 recall therefore increased from **9.21% to 13.16%**, while precision increased from **5.51% to 7.87%**.

At 5% capacity, C4 recall increased from **19.74% to 21.05%**, and at 10% capacity it increased from **27.63% to 28.95%**.

At broader inspection capacities, the two approaches performed similarly, with the baseline slightly outperforming the hybrid ranking at 7.5% and 20%.

These findings suggest that temporal reasoning provides its greatest value as a **high-priority triage mechanism**, helping distinguish the most urgent vehicles when maintenance resources are limited, rather than universally replacing the underlying ML risk ranking.

### 5. Lift Under Limited Inspection Capacity

The validation fleet contains 76 C4 vehicles among 5,046 vehicles, corresponding to a C4 prevalence of approximately **1.51%**.

At 2.5% inspection capacity, the hybrid system achieved a C4 precision of **7.87%**. This corresponds to approximately:

**Lift@2.5% = 7.87% / 1.51% ≈ 5.2×**

Therefore, a vehicle selected within the highest-priority 2.5% by the hybrid system was approximately **5.2 times as likely to be a C4 vehicle as a randomly selected vehicle**.

This provides an operationally interpretable measure of the system's ability to concentrate critical maintenance cases into a limited inspection budget.

### 6. Overall Evaluation

The evaluation highlights an important distinction between **classification performance and maintenance decision support**.

Exact multiclass prediction remains challenging because repair-proximity classes are extremely imbalanced, particularly C1–C3. As a result, overall accuracy is not used as the primary indicator of system effectiveness.

Instead, the predictive model provides useful risk-ranking signals, particularly for near-term Risk6, while the temporal reasoning layer incorporates historical persistence, deterioration and fleet-relative evidence to convert those signals into interpretable maintenance actions.

The final evaluation indicates that the hybrid system is particularly useful when inspection capacity is highly constrained. At 2.5% fleet capacity, temporal reasoning increased C4 recall from 9.21% to 13.16% and increased precision from 5.51% to 7.87%. At broader capacities, performance was broadly comparable to the raw ML ranking.

Overall, the results support FleetCare AI primarily as a **maintenance prioritization and decision-support system rather than a standalone high-accuracy multiclass classifier**. The predictive model identifies risk, while the temporal reasoning layer adds longitudinal context, explainability, and actionable maintenance prioritization.
